Research

Focus Areas

BIMM Lab studies how fluid-filled biological structures — vascular tissue, cellular fluids, layered membranes — can inform the design of engineered composites that do more than one job at once. Our work spans materials mechanics, spaceflight engineering, and structural design.

Fluid-Filled Composite Materials

We study composite structures with engineered fluid-filled channels and layers, characterizing how fluid content and distribution change stiffness, damping, and load response compared to conventional solid laminates. This work builds analytical and experimental tools for predicting the mechanical behavior of these multifunctional systems.

Radiation Shielding

Fluid-filled composites offer a route to combine structural function with attenuation of ionizing radiation, an important requirement for crewed spaceflight and other high-radiation environments. The lab investigates material configurations and fluid chemistries suited to this dual role.

Space Habitat Protection

Extending our materials work to habitat-scale structures, we explore how multifunctional composite panels could contribute to protecting crewed space habitats from radiation and micrometeoroid impacts while carrying structural load.

Biomimetic Multifunctional Materials Design

More broadly, the lab looks to biological precedent — vascular networks, layered tissue, and natural heat-transfer strategies — for design principles that can be translated into engineered systems, including biomimetic heat pipe and radiator concepts for spaceflight thermal management.
