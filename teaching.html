Teaching

Courses taught by Dr. Ghosh in the Solid Mechanics stream of the Mechanical Engineering Department, spanning graduate composite mechanics through undergraduate machine design and engineering economics.

Graduate

MENG 583 / EM 589

Engineering Mechanics of Composite Structures

This course addresses issues related to composite structures, their macro-mechanics to structural design and development. Emphasis is given to development of analytical procedures for determining material properties and prediction of structural behavior. Development of effective experimental methods is also looked into for material characterization, stress analysis, and failure analysis using Maximum Stress criterion and the Tsai-Wu criterion.

Undergraduate

MENG 451

Design of Machine Elements

Capstone class in the Solid Mechanics stream. The purpose of this class is to integrate course material learnt in Statics (ES 201), Mechanics of Materials (ES 302), Materials Engineering (MATE 202), and Advanced Mechanics of Materials (MENG 304). In addition, there are concepts of “Mathematical Statistics” that need to be understood before one can design machine elements properly. Course content is delivered in a “System’s Approach” through the design of a “Gear Box.”

ES 316

Engineering Economics

The course starts with an introduction to Professional Ethics, followed by topics including: economic decision-making for engineering alternatives; use of simple and compound interest calculations to compare the relative economy of investments and procedures; application of economic principles such as return on investment, replacement analysis, depreciation, uncertainty, income tax, and economic analysis of the public sector.
